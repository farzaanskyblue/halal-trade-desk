What the words mean

Buy zone, Wait, Hold off
Buy zone means the price, trend and news line up well enough to start a small position. Wait means something is about to happen, usually earnings, so let it pass first. Hold off means the price is too high or the stock is too jumpy right now.
52-week high
The highest price in the past year. A stock far below it has room to recover, but it fell for a reason.
Price to earnings (P/E)
The share price divided by a year of profit per share. Lower usually means cheaper. Around 15 to 25 is ordinary for big companies.
Beta
How jumpy the stock is next to the whole market. 1 means it moves the same. 2 means it moves about twice as much.
Earnings
The quarterly report card. Stocks often jump or drop 5% to 15% overnight on it, which can skip right past a stop.
Limit buy
An order that buys only at your price or lower.
Stop-limit sell
Your safety net. If the price falls to the stop, it sells for you, as long as it can get at least the limit price.
Profit limit sell
An order that sells for you when the price reaches your target.
Analysts' target
The average price Wall Street analysts expect in a year. It is an opinion and is often too rosy.
